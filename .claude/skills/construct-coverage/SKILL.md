# The coverage lens

## Posture

What a collection leaves out is a claim it makes without saying so: the job
is naming the frame, the absences inside it, and which absences are the
record being silent rather than the world being empty.

## When this applies

Take this lens when the work touches coverage-gaps.

## The questions

Work through every one. A question left unasked is a finding not made, and
the answer "nothing found" is only worth reading once the question has been
put.

1. What is the intended frame — the population, geography, or period this is
   meant to cover — and is it written down anywhere a reader can see?
2. Where is the collection empty, and for each empty region: is that because
   nothing happened there, because nothing was recorded, or because nothing
   has been collected yet? Those three are indistinguishable to a reader and
   must not be to the work.
3. Whose record is systematically thinner here, and does the collection
   method explain why? A method that reaches institutions reaches what
   institutions kept.
4. What coverage does the surface imply to someone who reads it without
   reading the method, and is that implication true?
5. What would have to be measured to state coverage as a number rather than
   as an impression, and does that measurement exist?

## What the deliverable must carry

### coverage-gaps — review memo

Stages, in order: discover, research, clarify, draft, review.

Every one of these is filled before the work is finished; a fact the
material cannot settle is written as an assumption, never left blank:

- finding — the conclusion, stated first, in plain language
- evidence — what supports the finding, each item citing a source read or
  the domain catalog
- risks — what could make the finding wrong, or "none identified" said
  explicitly
- coverage-frame — the intended frame in one sentence, and what falls
  outside it on purpose
- absences — each known absence, marked as not-recorded, not-yet-collected,
  or did-not-happen, with what distinguishes it

Filled when there is something to say:

- open-questions — what remains unknown, each with the assumed default the
  draft proceeds on

## When to stop and escalate

- An absence that cannot be classified from the material: put it in the
  decision inbox as an open question, never as a coverage claim.
- A surface that implies completeness the collection does not have: raise it
  as a public-claim finding, not a documentation task.

## Limits

this lens reports the shape of what is missing; it does not fill gaps, and
an absence it cannot classify stays classified as unknown

## What this method stands on

References identify where the discipline comes from; they are not reproduced
here, and what a standard currently says is checked against the standard.

- Total Survey Error: Past, Present, and Future (Public Opinion Quarterly
  74(5), 2010) (Groves and Lyberg, Oxford University Press) — coverage error
  as a named, separable component of representation error, distinct from
  sampling and non-response
- Datasheets for Datasets (Communications of the ACM, December 2021) (Gebru,
  Morgenstern, Vecchione, Wortman Vaughan, Wallach, Daumé III, and Crawford)
  — the documentation questions a collection owes its reader: motivation,
  composition, collection process, and what it should not be used for
- Silencing the Past: Power and the Production of History (1995)
  (Michel-Rolph Trouillot, Beacon Press) — the account of how absences enter
  a record at the moments of making, archiving, retrieval, and retelling,
  which is why an empty region is read as a claim rather than as nothing
