# Vesper digitization pilot decision brief

**Decision status:** Recommended with launch controls  
**Pilot window:** November 2026  
**Evidence current through:** 2026-10-09

## Recommendation

Run an internal-only, timeboxed pilot using a preselected queue from the 120 photographs with recorded pilot clearance. Use the 12 total operator-hours and the shared scanner to validate the complete workflow: selection, setup, capture, metadata, quality control, checksum, provenance, rework, storage, and closeout. Define success by accepted outputs and useful operating evidence, not by a promised object count. [cite: archive://catalog/batch-7] [cite: archive://operations/current] [cite: archive://rights/summary]

Proceed only after both launch gates are met:

1. The authorized rights owner confirms that the selected photograph queue and proposed internal storage workflow fall within the recorded pilot clearance.
2. A preservation owner approves a temporary storage, backup, access, retention, and disposition rule for the pilot files.

This is the most feasible next-month option. Including notebooks would require a conservator-approved capture method, while audio requires a separate playback and capture workflow. [cite: archive://conservation/handling]

## Pilot scope

### In scope

- A representative queue selected only from the 120 photographs with internal pilot clearance. [cite: archive://catalog/batch-7] [cite: archive://rights/summary]
- No more than the 12 total operator-hours already allocated across the two trained operators using one shared scanner. [cite: archive://operations/current]
- Preservation masters with a stable object ID, documented capture settings, checksum, and source-object provenance. [cite: archive://operations/quality]
- Quality checks for focus, completeness, orientation, and checksum verification. [cite: archive://operations/quality]
- Measurement of accepted-object throughput, rework, time by workflow stage, and representative output storage.
- An internal closeout record that supports a later scale/no-scale decision.

### Out of scope

- The other photographs whose rights review is incomplete. [cite: archive://catalog/batch-7]
- Bound notebooks, because their brittle bindings cannot use the flatbed scanner and no supported method has been selected. [cite: archive://conservation/handling]
- Audio reels, which require a separate playback and capture workflow. [cite: archive://conservation/handling]
- Public release or access derivatives. Public distribution rights are unsettled, and preservation capture does not authorize publication. [cite: docs/collection-policy.md] [cite: archive://rights/summary]
- A commitment to a final object count. The observed rate of 15 photographs per hour includes basic quality checks but is not guaranteed capacity; current operations explicitly says not to promise 180 final objects. [cite: archive://operations/current]

## Evidence and confidence

The evidence consists of current project policy and source-specific archive records. These are treated as **records** for their stated purposes; the recommendation itself is an **inference** from those records.

- **Eligibility:** The current inventory lists 600 photographs, 40 bound notebooks, and 15 audio reels; only a 120-photograph subset has recorded internal pilot clearance. [cite: archive://catalog/batch-7] [cite: archive://rights/summary]
- **Capacity:** Two trained operators share one scanner, with 12 operator-hours total allocated next month. [cite: archive://operations/current]
- **Readiness:** Notebook and audio workflows are not ready for this scanner-based pilot. [cite: archive://conservation/handling]
- **Quality:** Current requirements cover identifiers, capture settings, checksums, provenance, focus, completeness, and orientation. [cite: archive://operations/quality]
- **Preservation gap:** Storage estimates require actual sample outputs, while repository retention and long-term ownership remain unapproved. [cite: archive://operations/quality] [cite: archive://preservation/retention]
- **Rights boundary:** Internal pilot clearance is recorded for the subset, but public distribution is not settled. The donor deed could not be read with current access, so no deed terms or legal conclusion are inferred. [cite: archive://rights/summary] [unverified: archive://rights/donor-deed requires review by authorized collections staff.]

**Confidence:** High that the controlled pilot can start in November once the launch gates are met. Medium on its final accepted-object count and storage requirement because those are the measurements the pilot must produce.

The superseded summer estimate claimed the whole collection could be scanned by one operator in a week and published immediately. It is not current evidence: the estimate was withdrawn, current capacity is 12 total hours, some object classes are not scanner-ready, and public rights are unresolved. ## Risks and controls

1. **Files are captured without an accountable preservation destination.** This could leave technically valid files unmanaged after the pilot. **Control:** make the named preservation owner and temporary storage/backup/access/disposition rule a launch gate. [cite: archive://operations/quality] [cite: archive://preservation/retention]
2. **Internal clearance is interpreted too broadly.** The selected queue or storage workflow could exceed the recorded permission. **Control:** require written confirmation from the authorized rights owner before capture; keep public release out of scope. [cite: archive://rights/summary]
3. **The calibration rate becomes an output promise.** Metadata, QC, and rework could then be squeezed out. **Control:** retain the 12-hour ceiling, do not set a promised object count, and accept only complete records. [cite: archive://operations/current] [cite: archive://operations/quality]
4. **The sample is too uniform to inform scaling.** A fast but unrepresentative queue could understate rework or storage. **Control:** have collections staff preselect a representative range of cleared photographs and record selection criteria.
5. **Closeout is skipped.** The pilot could produce files without resolving whether to scale. **Control:** reserve time within the existing allocation for reconciliation, metrics, and a documented scale/no-scale recommendation.

## Success criteria

The pilot succeeds if it stays within the approved queue and 12-hour ceiling, every accepted master meets the recorded metadata and QC requirements, all outputs are reconciled to source objects and checksums, storage and rework are measured, and a closeout identifies the actual bottleneck and the conditions for expansion. It does not fail merely because it produces fewer objects than the calibration rate might suggest.

## Next actions

1. **Rights owner:** confirm the selected photograph queue and internal storage workflow are within the recorded pilot clearance; keep the restricted donor deed review with authorized staff.
2. **Preservation lead:** accept ownership of pilot outputs and approve temporary storage, backup, access, retention, and disposition before capture.
3. **Collections staff:** select and document a representative queue from the cleared subset; exclude notebooks, audio, and uncleared photographs.
4. **Operators:** reserve the shared scanner and the 12 total operator-hours, including setup, metadata, QC, rework, and closeout.
5. **Pilot lead:** prepare the object-level manifest and acceptance checklist before the first capture.
6. **Pilot lead:** at closeout, report accepted objects, rejected or reworked objects, time by stage, measured storage, exceptions, and a scale/no-scale recommendation.

## Challenge record

**Verdict: Accepted with controls.** The strongest failure mode is producing valid files without approved ownership or retention. The best alternative, a no-capture workflow rehearsal, avoids that risk but cannot measure real throughput or storage and therefore does not answer the key scaling questions. The recommendation remains viable only with the two launch gates above. This challenge was a self-review using the same evidence and may share the drafter's blind spots.
