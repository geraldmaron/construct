## Resolving the catalogue conflict

The catalogue's `acquisition_year` is 1931. Its included dictionary says this legacy field records first receipt or old-catalogue entry, including loans, rather than legal title transfer. Inference: the year is consistent with the intake receipt and does not contradict the later accepted transfer. A current export of a legacy field does not make that field evidence of an earlier purchase. [cite: B6-catalogue-export.txt; B2-intake-receipt.txt; B3-transfer-agreement.txt; B4-trustee-minute.txt]

Inference: these records concern the same object because the brass plate NV-17 links the receipt, sale, acceptance, location entries and catalogue; the receipt and catalogue also share the crescent-shaped repair, and the sale and acceptance refer back to loan L31.6. The catalogue links NV-17 to accession P34.22. This identity chain reduces the risk of assigning another object's history to the brochure photograph. [cite: B1-brochure-and-press.txt; B2-intake-receipt.txt; B3-transfer-agreement.txt; B4-trustee-minute.txt; B5-location-register.txt; B6-catalogue-export.txt]

Inference: the signed receipt is direct evidence of custody terms; the executed sale and signed acceptance address the transfer condition; the gallery register addresses particular display events. The brochure is an unsupported restatement on these points, and the catalogue has narrower field semantics. Authority is specific to each claim. [cite: B1-brochure-and-press.txt; B2-intake-receipt.txt; B3-transfer-agreement.txt; B4-trustee-minute.txt; B5-location-register.txt; B6-catalogue-export.txt]

## Disconfirmation

Two initially plausible hypotheses are **H1**, the brochure compressed first receipt, later purchase and intermittent display into one origin story, and **H2**, “acquisition” referred to a real earlier purchase, while the later sale and gallery movements were administrative formalities. Both can explain a brochure and a catalogue carrying the early year before the detailed records are checked; neither establishes the author's intent. [cite: B1-brochure-and-press.txt; B6-catalogue-export.txt]

To refute H1, I looked for a purchase instrument at arrival, unconditional title transfer before the trustee meeting, a separate chest identity, or language indicating continued public view during the recorded storage. The receipt, sale clause, acceptance, identifiers and explicit “not on public view” entry oppose those possibilities. To refute H2, I checked whether the loan terms retained title, whether acceptance had substantive effect under the actual agreement, and whether storage explicitly removed public access. Each check contradicts H2. This was a pass through the supplied texts; no outside search was performed. [cite: B2-intake-receipt.txt; B3-transfer-agreement.txt; B4-trustee-minute.txt; B5-location-register.txt; B6-catalogue-export.txt]
