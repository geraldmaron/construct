# Archive pilot: feasibility assessment

Assessment date: October 9, 2026. Scope: the proposed photograph collection and its scanning/publication plan, assessed solely against the three held records. No photograph inventory, donor deed, underlying throughput log, or approval instrument was supplied. This is a records-based feasibility finding, not a legal opinion.

## Finding

**Inference: the proposal is not feasible as written on the held evidence.** It overstates allocated operator time, cannot reach its stated collection size even on its own calculation, and proposes public distribution where the held rights record reports unresolved rights and no legal approval. Calling the proposal “final” does not establish approval. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

**Inference: a smaller, internal-only pilot is conditionally plausible, not demonstrated ready.** The cleared internal scope fits the scan-only arithmetic, but the records do not establish whether the remaining time covers setup and metadata, whether the sampled rate transfers to those photographs, or which items are cleared. The evidence supports revising the proposal for validation, not approving the existing public-release plan. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Evidence and source status

- **Record: proposal.md** is the proposal itself. It requests scanning and publicly publishing all 600 photographs “next month,” assumes two operators with 12 hours each, and calculates 360 scans at 15 photographs per hour. It calls itself final for approval, not approved. [cite: proposal.md:1]
- **Record: operations.md** is the held allocation/operations statement, not an underlying time log. It explicitly corrects the allocation to 12 operator-hours total shared by two staff. It reports a sampled rate of 15 photographs per hour excluding setup and metadata. [cite: operations.md:1]
- **Record: rights.md** is the held rights-status statement, not the donor deed or a clearance instrument. It reports only 120 photographs cleared for an internal pilot, unresolved public distribution rights, an unavailable restricted donor deed, and no legal approval. These are reported statuses, not independently verified legal conclusions. [cite: rights.md:1]

All three records are undated. “Current” is the operations record's status description, not evidence of an October allocation; “next month” is the proposal's relative target, not an established calendar deadline. **Inference:** translating it into November 2026 would require an unsupported assumption about when the proposal was written. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1]

## Capacity reconciliation

The following are **inferences calculated from the held figures**, assuming the sampled rate is sustained. They are scan-only planning estimates, not measured end-to-end capacity. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1]

| Scenario | Calculation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposal's assumed staffing | 2 × 12 = 24 operator-hours; 24 × 15 = 360 photographs | Even its own staffing premise leaves 240 of the proposed 600 unscanned. [cite: proposal.md:1] |
| Actual recorded allocation | 12 × 15 = 180 photographs | Scan-only estimate is half the proposal's estimate, before setup and metadata consume time. [cite: operations.md:1; proposal.md:1] |
| Entire proposed collection | 600 ÷ 15 = 40 scan-hours | Requires 28 more operator-hours than allocated, before excluded work. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1] |
| Cleared internal pilot | 120 ÷ 15 = 8 scan-hours; 12 − 8 = 4 hours | Leaves at most four allocated hours for setup, metadata, and any other required work under the rate assumption. [cite: rights.md:1; operations.md:1] |

**Inference:** two people do not double a shared operator-hour budget. Parallel work may affect elapsed time, but does not establish additional labor capacity. Nor is the sampled rate a proven hard ceiling: faster performance could change the estimates, but no held evidence demonstrates it. Scanning completion would still not prove publication readiness. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Disconfirmation pass

Two hypotheses were tested against every held record:

- **H1: the exact public-release proposal is unsupported under recorded conditions.** Refuters sought: a larger allocation, a demonstrated rate sufficient for the whole collection, or public-release clearance. The operations and rights records instead expressly contradict those premises. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
- **H2: the proposal may be viable because the held set omits newer staffing and rights decisions.** Refuters sought: evidence establishing the held records as complete and contemporaneous. None establishes that; however, no held record supplies the missing decisions either. This rival remains possible outside the record, not a basis for approval from it. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

| Evidence | H1: unsupported on recorded conditions | H2: viable through omitted newer decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposal calculates fewer scans than its requested total. [cite: proposal.md:1] | Consistent | Inconsistent with the written plan unless it is revised |
| Operations expressly says total hours, not hours each. [cite: operations.md:1] | Consistent | Silent about later changes; contradicts reliance on the held allocation |
| Rights statement limits internal clearance and reports unresolved public rights. [cite: rights.md:1] | Consistent | Silent about later decisions; contradicts reliance on held clearance |
| Records are undated. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1] | Limits claims about present-day conditions | Consistent with possible incompleteness, but proves no update |

**Inference:** the strongest available disconfirmation does not overturn H1. It narrows the finding to feasibility *from held records*, rather than impossibility under every future arrangement. The internal-clearance statement also prevents an overbroad conclusion that no pilot at all could proceed. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Coverage, absences, and independence

The proposed population is all 600 photographs; the documented internal-clearance subset is 120. **Inference:** the remaining 480 are outside the recorded internal clearance, not proven permanently prohibited or uncleareable. No item-level inventory establishes whether the cleared subset represents the broader collection. [cite: proposal.md:1; rights.md:1]

- **Not recorded in the held set:** proposal date, absolute deadline, item identities, sample composition and variability, setup/metadata durations, and publication workflow requirements. These are unknown, not evidence that the work or records do not exist. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
- **Not yet collected here:** the restricted donor deed is expressly unavailable; its terms cannot be reconstructed from the rights summary. [cite: rights.md:1]
- **Did not happen, as reported:** legal approval does not exist according to the rights statement. This is an explicit negative statement, not an inference from an absent attachment; its effective date remains unknown. [cite: rights.md:1]
- **Inference, coverage skew:** evidence is thinner for photographs outside the internally cleared subset and for donor-imposed conditions. The records cannot support conclusions about whose subjects, donors, or communities are over- or underrepresented. [cite: rights.md:1; proposal.md:1]

## Strongest objection

**Inference:** the proposal might be seeking extra resources and future clearance rather than claiming readiness today. That interpretation could support a revised plan, but the proposal neither specifies those dependencies nor reconciles its own capacity shortfall. The operations correction and rights restriction are the strongest held counterevidence to treating it as approval-ready. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

The strongest failure mode of narrowing the plan is mistaking internal clearance plus scan arithmetic for end-to-end readiness. The best alternative is to defer execution until item identification and a timed workflow check settle those gaps. **Assessment: needs validation**, not accepted as ready. This conclusion concerns evidentiary sufficiency, not an authorization decision. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Unresolved issues and evidence needed

1. **Which photographs are cleared, and on what internal-use terms?** Obtain the item-level clearance register and restricted donor deed from the records/rights custodian. The held status statement supplies a count, not identities or terms; access is needed to settle this. [cite: rights.md:1]
2. **Can the internal workflow fit the allocation?** Obtain the original sample log and time a representative cleared batch including setup and metadata, plus any required handling, checking, and storage work. The held rate expressly excludes part of the workload. Completion within the four-hour residual allowance is unverified and requires measurement. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
3. **What is the actual deadline and committed staffing?** Obtain a dated proposal and confirmed allocation. The held answer is 12 total operator-hours, not 24; only an authorized update could change it. The relative deadline cannot be resolved from these undated records. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1]
4. **Can public distribution be authorized?** The current held answer is unresolved, with no legal approval. An authorized rights review using the missing deed and other applicable clearance records must settle public use; an internal pilot cannot establish that permission. [cite: rights.md:1]

### Pre-mortem

**Inference:** the likely failure story for a revised internal pilot is that scan-only estimates are treated as a complete schedule, excluded work exhausts the remaining time, and internal clearance is later mistaken for public-release permission. Validation must keep end-to-end timing and release authorization separate; neither gap is closed by the held records. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
