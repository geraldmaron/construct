# Archive pilot: held-record feasibility assessment

## Outcome and recommendation

**Inference: reject the proposal as written.** Scanning and publicly publishing the full collection is not supported by the held staffing allocation or rights record. The proposal overstates operator-hours; even its own scanning estimate falls short of its target. Public distribution remains unresolved, independently of capacity. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

**Recommendation: consider a revised, internal-only pilot limited to the cleared photographs, subject to validating total effort and obtaining a revised scope decision. Status: needs validation, not approved for execution.** This is a conditional alternative, not a finding that the full internal pilot can definitely finish within the allocation. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

This assessment covers only the proposed photograph collection and the three held records; it does not establish legal rights independently, inspect the photographs, or test a production workflow. All three substantive files were opened in full. No external lookup was used: public information cannot establish the missing private deed, internal allocation, or approval. Assessment date: October 9, 2026 (session date). The proposal's “next month” is an undated relative schedule, not a verified calendar deadline. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Capacity reconciliation

All calculations below use photographs and **aggregate operator-hours**, not elapsed hours or hours per staff member. The population is the proposed collection; the time basis is the allocation described as current, applied to the proposed work window for this comparison. Throughput is a sampled scanning rate, excluding setup and metadata, not an end-to-end completion rate. Derived quantities are inferences conditional on that sample being applicable. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1]

| Corrected scan-only capacity | 12 aggregate operator-hours × 15 photographs/hour = 180 photographs | Short by 420 photographs; covers 180/600 = 30% of the target before overhead. This is a conditional scan-only ceiling, not a guaranteed output. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1] |

| Internal-pilot effort | 120 cleared photographs ÷ 15 photographs/hour = 8 operator-hours; 12 − 8 = 4 | At the sampled rate, 4 operator-hours remain for all other required pilot work. **Inference:** adding a second operator does not double a shared allocation. Net completed output cannot be calculated because setup and metadata effort are unquantified, and public-publication effort is not recorded. Neither the sampled rate nor the conditional remaining time proves completion by the proposal's deadline. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1]

## Rights and coverage limits

**Inference:** internal-pilot clearance must not be treated as public-distribution clearance. The record supports no verified public-release quantity; that is not a finding that no public rights exist. The unavailable deed leaves their extent unresolved, while the rights record explicitly says no legal approval exists. [cite: rights.md:1]

Notable absences are classified as follows:

- **Not-yet-collected in this assessment:** the restricted donor deed is explicitly unavailable in the fixture. Its absence is an access limit, not proof of its contents. [cite: rights.md:1]
- **Not-recorded:** setup and metadata effort, item-level clearance identifiers, underlying throughput observations, an absolute deadline, and publication workflow effort are absent from the held files. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
- **Did-not-happen, as reported only:** legal approval has not occurred according to the rights statement; no independent approval register was inspected. [cite: rights.md:1]

**Inference:** clearance evidence is systematically thinner for photographs outside the internally cleared subset; no item-level donor or subject coverage is supplied, so no demographic or provenance representativeness claim is possible. [cite: proposal.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Disconfirmation pass

Two hypotheses fit at least part of the evidence: **H1**, the proposal is infeasible under the held conditions; **H2**, incomplete or outdated records conceal sufficient resources and public permissions. H1 would be refuted by a superseding allocation, measured end-to-end capacity and applicable public clearance. H2 would be weakened by explicit operational correction and an affirmative absence of approval. I searched the full held inventory and read all substantive records for those refuters; no superseding evidence is held. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

| Evidence sought and found | H1: infeasible under held conditions | H2: sufficient conditions exist but are missing |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit shared-hours correction | Consistent | Inconsistent with treating the proposal's hours as current. [cite: operations.md:1] |
| Proposal's scan estimate below target | Consistent | Inconsistent without additional, unrecorded capacity. [cite: proposal.md:1] |
| Public rights unresolved; no legal approval | Consistent | Inconsistent with asserting approval now. [cite: rights.md:1] |
| Deed unavailable; no record dates | Silent on actual deed terms | Consistent with incomplete evidence, but not proof of permission. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1] |
| Internal subset cleared | Consistent with rejecting full public scope | Consistent only with a narrower alternative. [cite: rights.md:1] |

**Inference:** H1 survives the held-record disconfirmation better. The pass does not reverse the conclusion, but limits it to the held conditions rather than asserting impossibility under every future allocation or permission. [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Strongest objection

The strongest objection is that these short, undated records may omit a later allocation or applicable permissions in the deed. That could change feasibility. It cannot justify approval now: the explicit correction and unresolved public rights are the best held evidence, and the proposal is short of its target even on its own estimate. **Inference: reject the full public proposal; retain the internal alternative as conditional.** [cite: proposal.md:1; operations.md:1; rights.md:1]

## Unresolved issues and necessary decisions

1. **Public scope:** an authorized rights decision based on the restricted deed and other applicable permissions is required before public release can be supported. The held record cannot settle it because the deed is unavailable and public rights are unresolved. This blocks public distribution, not automatically the already-cleared internal subset. [cite: rights.md:1]
2. **Internal completion:** end-to-end effort for the cleared subset remains unverified. The missing prerequisite is evidence that scanning and all required non-scanning work fit the allocation; at the sampled rate, the latter has a 4-operator-hour allowance. The held sample excludes setup and metadata, so these files cannot establish that condition. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
3. **Scope decision:** the approving authority must choose whether to replace the full public proposal with the narrower internal pilot. The original request is for approval, not an approval already granted. The internally cleared item identities must also be available before execution; the held aggregate clearance does not identify them. [cite: proposal.md:1; rights.md:1]

No outreach, spending, rights change, scanning or publication is authorized or performed by this assessment. Resolving the absolute deadline alone would not remove the capacity and rights blockers.

## Pre-mortem for the internal alternative

**Inference:** the most evident failure story is treating the scan-only estimate as a completion promise, then exceeding available time on setup or metadata. Another is allowing an internal-only pilot to drift into public release. Controls are to retain the internal boundary and cleared-item scope, and validate total effort before promising completion. These are proposed conditions, not an approved operating plan. [cite: operations.md:1; rights.md:1]
