# Decision framing: when to author wave two

Scope check: this is a real choice between alternatives with weight behind it. Authoring time is cheap, but the scarce resource at stake (Gerald's usage bandwidth, the only source of falsification evidence) makes the sequencing consequential, so the method engages rather than standing down.

## Frame

**The decision, as a choice.** Choose when the three wave-two skill promotions (system-understanding, adversarial-review, deliverable-gate) get authored: now, while the wave-one authoring pattern is active, or on a later trigger tied to accumulated real use.

**Who decides.** Gerald decides. Consulted: nobody, by his own statement [cite: requester].

**Reversibility class: costly to reverse.** The authored files themselves are deletable at near-zero cost, which would argue reversible. But the decision's real consequences are not in the files: uses of the fixed bandwidth pool that get spread across more skills cannot be recalled, and a falsification test slowed by dilution has lost calendar time it cannot recover. The classification is uncertain, so per this method it is classed upward rather than down.

**Do-nothing baseline.** Wave two stays as recorded candidates. No authoring cost is paid and no bandwidth is diluted. Costs that accrue: the consistency window closes on its own schedule as wave one finishes (two skills remain unshipped [cite: requester]); the candidates' context decays in memory rather than in writing; and with no explicit trigger, the "author wave two?" question resurfaces ad hoc every few sessions, each time costing a re-deliberation. Do-nothing is close to a hold, but a hold with no gate and no capture, which is its worst version.

### Constraints (falsifiable, disqualifying)

1. Gerald's own usage is the only source of recorded uses; there are no external users to generate evidence [cite: requester].
2. Wave two is candidates, not filed work; any authoring path must include filing first [cite: requester].
3. The pilot's falsification test is pre-registered: refuted at ten real uses with zero gate-changed outcomes; currently one use, one gate-changed outcome [cite: requester]. Any option that would prevent that test from resolving is disqualified.

### Preferences (weights, not walls)

- Consistency of the authoring standard across skills [cite: requester].
- Avoiding a shelf of unused skills [cite: requester].
- Authoring time treated as cheap but not free [cite: requester].
- Faster resolution of falsification tests (inference: this follows from the tests being the project's evidence mechanism; the requester did not state it as a goal directly).

## Options

**Author wave two now.** File and author all three promotions immediately, while the standard is freshly exercised and the sessions doing the authoring are the same ones that shipped decision-framing and written-voice today. Consistency is at its maximum; the marginal cost per skill is at its minimum; the falsification tests exist precisely so that shipping ahead of demand is safe, because unused skills get caught and killed by their own pre-registered tests rather than by up-front gatekeeping.

**Evidence-gated hold.** Keep wave two as candidates until a stated gate fires: the pilot's ten-use test resolves, or each shipped wave-one skill shows recorded real use. (Both gate formulations are the requester's proposed lines, not external standards; "a few ledger entries" is a chosen threshold, chosen by Gerald, with no independent source [cite: requester].) Wave one's evidence accumulates undiluted; wave two is authored only once the method-skill format itself has demonstrated it changes outcomes beyond a single data point.

**Demand-pulled promotion.** Keep wave two as candidates, write a one-paragraph capture note per candidate now (scope, source location in the personal collection, what the falsification test would be), and author each skill the first time a real piece of work would actually invoke it. Authoring then arrives with a guaranteed first recorded use attached, so the shelfware failure mode is structurally impossible and no bandwidth is spent speculatively. The authoring standard document, plus wave one's two still-unshipped skills, carry the pattern's consistency in the meantime.

**Ungated drift (do-nothing).** Described above as the baseline. Viable in the sense that nothing breaks, but strictly dominated by demand-pulled promotion, which differs only by adding capture notes and a trigger at a cost of minutes. Listed, not recommended, and not disqualified by any constraint.

No option is removed by a constraint; all four are visible above.

## Trade-offs

The comparison is contested (author-now and hold each have a real case), so the table:

| Criterion | Author now | Evidence-gated hold | Demand-pulled | Ungated drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency of standard | Maximal; same-day pattern | Risk of drift by gate time, partly offset by wave one still finishing | Same offset; authoring standard is the written backstop | Same as demand-pulled, minus captured context |
| Falsification-evidence speed | Dilutes the pool across 7+ skills; slows every test, including the pilot's [assumed: wave-two skills would absorb uses that wave-one skills would otherwise get; this is an inference from "every skill added divides the same pool" [cite: requester], not a measured effect] | Undiluted until gate fires | Undiluted; each promotion arrives with its first use | Undiluted |
| Shelfware risk | Highest; three skills ship with zero demand evidence | Low; authored only after format shows value | Structurally zero; authoring is triggered by a use | Low, but candidates may silently die |
| Authoring cost timing | All paid now, cheapest per unit | Paid later, possibly with re-ramp cost | Paid later, amortized, small mid-task disruption per skill | Paid later or never |
| Candidate-context preservation | Perfect (context consumed immediately) | Decays unless captured | Captured now in notes | Decays |

One factual note the framing leans on: the "pattern is hot now or never" urgency is weaker than it first appears, because wave one itself has two skills left to author [cite: requester]. The consistency window is held open by wave one's own completion, independent of this decision. That is an inference from the wave-one status, and it is load-bearing for the recommendation.

## Recommendation

**Gerald: hold wave two, write one-paragraph capture notes for all three candidates now, and author each skill at its first real point of use rather than on a count gate or a calendar.**

This takes the hold side of the binary you posed, but replaces the count-gate trigger with a demand trigger, because a demand trigger converts your central fear (shelfware) from a risk to be monitored into an outcome that cannot occur, and it spends your scarce bandwidth only where a use was going to happen anyway. The capture notes, costing minutes, neutralize the main cost of holding (context decay), and the authoring standard plus wave one's remaining two skills keep the pattern warm through the near term.

### Strongest objection, in its own words

"You built pre-registered falsification tests precisely so you would not have to gatekeep on prediction. The whole point of that machinery is that shipping ahead of demand is cheap and safe: an unused skill gets refuted by its own test and dies with a clean record, which is itself valuable information about the collection. Holding wave two is redundant caution layered on top of a mechanism designed to make that caution unnecessary, and it pre-judges exactly the question the tests exist to adjudicate. Meanwhile you pay a real price: the one moment when three skills could be authored in a single consistent pass, by the same hands that just wrote the standard, and you are letting it go for a dilution effect you have inferred but never measured."

The answer to it, briefly: falsification tests only adjudicate if they resolve, and resolution runs on the same divided bandwidth pool; a mechanism that catches shelfware slowly is paid for in the calendar time of every other test. But the objection's charge that the dilution effect is unmeasured is correct, and it is marked as such above.

### What would change the answer

- The pilot's ten-use test resolves either way: refuted would argue the whole promotion program pause harder; survived would license batch-authoring wave two with real evidence behind the format.
- A measured counter-observation to the dilution assumption: if ledger entries show wave-one skills accumulating uses at an undiminished rate after any new skill ships, the bandwidth-division premise weakens and author-in-batches gets cheaper.
- Wave one completes and weeks pass with no wave-two skill hitting a demand trigger: at that point the capture notes are the only thing holding context, and a deliberate batch-author session becomes the better consistency play than waiting longer.

## Pre-mortem

**The failure story.** The hold is adopted. Wave one finishes; real work, it turns out, rarely presents a moment that obviously calls for system-understanding or deliverable-gate, so no demand trigger ever cleanly fires; the triggers were vaguer than they felt when written. Months pass. The authoring pattern has gone cold, the capture notes turn out to be thinner than the tacit context they were supposed to replace, and when wave two is finally authored it is inconsistent with wave one and needs a rework pass. The hold bought no information, cost the consistency it was supposed to be cheap against, and the recorded-candidate list became the very shelf it was meant to prevent, just a shelf of notes instead of skills.

**Early signal.** Three or four weeks after wave one completes with zero demand triggers fired and zero new wave-one ledger entries: that is the signal the demand-pull mechanism is stalling, and it maps to the third revisit trigger above.

**Cheap mitigation now.** Two: write the capture notes immediately (already a control in the recommendation), and write each candidate's demand trigger as a falsifiable sentence ("author adversarial-review the next time a load-bearing decision is finalized in a session") rather than a vibe, so a stalled trigger is detectable instead of deniable.

**Did the pre-mortem weaken the recommendation?** Yes, at the margin. It exposed that vague demand triggers are the recommendation's weak joint, which is why the falsifiable-trigger wording was added as a named control and a time-boxed revisit was added to the triggers. The recommendation stands with those controls; without them it would be softer than it reads.
