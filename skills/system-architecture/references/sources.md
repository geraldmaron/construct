# Sources: System architecture

Doctrine this pack rests on. Each entry names what it is used for and when it was last reviewed. Each entry was opened on 2026-10-17 and says what was confirmed; a finding that leans on a specific clause or number still opens that clause. Time-sensitive doctrine carries a review-due date.

- Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions), and the ADR conventions at https://adr.github.io/. Used for: the record shape (context, decision, consequences) and immutability of past records. Reviewed: 2026-09-02; stable. Opened 2026-10-17: Nygard's 2011 post gives the sections title, context, decision, status, and consequences, and says a reversed decision is kept and marked superseded rather than edited; adr.github.io links that post as the one that popularized ADRs.
- The C4 model (https://c4model.com/). Used for: naming the level a diagram speaks at so boundary claims are compared like with like. Reviewed: 2026-09-02; stable. Opened 2026-10-17: Simon Brown's site defines four levels (system context, container, component, code) plus supporting landscape, dynamic, and deployment diagrams.
- AWS Well-Architected Framework pillars (https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/well-architected/). Used for: the reliability, security, and operational-excellence questions a design must answer; vendor-neutral reading. Reviewed: 2026-09-02, review due 2027-03. Opened 2026-10-17: the page names six pillars: operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimization, and sustainability.
- Ford, Parsons, Kua, Building Evolutionary Architectures (ISBN 978-1491986363) (https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9781491986363.json). Used for: fitness functions as the way an architectural property is measured rather than asserted. Reviewed: 2026-09-02; stable. Opened 2026-10-17: the Open Library record confirms the title, the three authors, O'Reilly Media 2017, and this ISBN; the publisher page refused automated access and the chapters on fitness functions were not opened.
- Conway, How Do Committees Invent? (1968) (https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html). Used for: reading team boundaries as design forces. Reviewed: 2026-09-02; stable. Opened 2026-10-17: the author's site carries the paper, published in Datamation, April 1968, arguing that a system's structure mirrors the communication structure of the organization that designs it.
- Skelton and Pais, Team Topologies (ISBN 978-1942788812) (https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9781942788812.json). Used for: cognitive load and ownership as boundary criteria. Reviewed: 2026-09-02; stable. Opened 2026-10-17: the Open Library record confirms the title, Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais as authors, IT Revolution Press 2019, and this ISBN; the book's content was not opened.
