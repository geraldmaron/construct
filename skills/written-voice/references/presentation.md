# Presentation rules

How a document looks decides whether its content is absorbed. Each rule
names whether it rests on evidence, convention, or house taste - a
formatting rule dressed as science is the kind of claim the claims
discipline catches.

## Standard header

Every document opens with the same fields in the same order (decision-log
entries skip it - see genre shapes):

```
<Title>
Status: draft | proposed | accepted | final | superseded
Author: <who wrote it> - Contributors: <everyone else who shaped it;
  models are contributors and are named honestly>
Created: <YYYY-MM-DD> - Last updated: <YYYY-MM-DD>
Tags: <three to six, lowercase, chosen for retrieval>
```

Status comes right after the title. Where the document carries a verdict,
status uses the verdict vocabulary. Last-updated is under the claims
discipline: content changed while the date stood still is a false claim
about freshness.

## Shape follows content

- Tables for discrete, comparable facts a reader will scan for a value
  (evidence: pre-registered randomized trial on tabular fact boxes vs
  prose).
- Numbered lists when sequence or step-reference matters, bulleted when
  not (convention).
- Prose for causal reasoning and narrative - reflexively listifying helps
  re-readers, not first-pass comprehension (evidence, with that condition).
- A page that is all one shape is a smell (house judgment).

## Segment and signal

Long continuous material is broken into headed chunks; each heading states
what its section says (evidence: two independent methodologies). The point
of a section lives in its first line (evidence-supported, with scanning
studies' descriptive-not-law caveat). No literal items-per-list ceiling as
science - the famous seven-item limit is a memory-span statistic from the
wrong domain; cap lengths for scanability as a design choice and say so.

Walls of text are a failure of segmentation, not word count. Scroll the
finished document; any screenful with no entry point gets one (house rule
serving the segmenting evidence).

## Visuals

A diagram where content is concrete or comparative beats describing it
(picture-superiority effect). Hand-drawn / sketch-style is the house
default for drafts and thinking documents (conditional evidence); avoid
where the artifact must read as authoritative or quantities must be judged
precisely (same research line).

## Reads human

Vary sentence length on purpose; uniform rhythm is a documented machine-text
marker. Banned lexical tics with measured frequency spikes: delve,
underscore, meticulous, intricate, commendable, realm. Formulaic
transitions ("moreover," "it's important to note") and the reflexive
rule-of-three go too (taste). Em dashes limited to rare deliberate use -
house taste only; no science claim.
